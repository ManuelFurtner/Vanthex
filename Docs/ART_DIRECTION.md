# VANTHEX — Art direction

Visual Pass 03. GRID uses a deeper modular kit, metal floor plates, overhead ducts, and a ballistic rifle. It is closer to a commercial blockout than Pass 02. It is not the high-fidelity reference.

The goal is a believable near-future tactical facility that stays readable in a competitive FPS. The greybox collision is the skeleton. Visual detail is built around it.

## Visual identity

VANTHEX is realistic, tactical, industrial, and dark, without becoming a black screen. The setting is about 30 to 60 years ahead. Technology should look like something a research site could actually build: reinforced concrete, painted steel, brushed metal, rubber, glass, cable trays, pipes, vents, racks, and cabinets.

Do not copy another game's maps, props, or logos. Do not drift into colorful hero-shooter science fiction. The place should look used and functional, not sterile and not theatrical.

## GRID

GRID is a secure underground Vanthex Energy research and containment hall.

- Alpha deploys from the south end. Bravo deploys from the north end. The two ends stay small.
- The experimental core sits in the center, off the main shot lane.
- Extraction A (northwest) and Extraction B (southeast) are transfer stations, not abstract pads.
- The left side keeps the raised catwalk.
- Cover, route walls, spawns, and the core home stay where gameplay put them.

Story the building should suggest: this site was made to study and move a valuable energy source. Signs say GRID, ALPHA, BRAVO, CORE, and TRANSFER A / TRANSFER B. Markings are original. No borrowed logos.

## Material language

Use a few shared materials, not a unique material per prop.

| Family | Role | Roughness / metal |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Concrete | Floor, walls, ceiling | High roughness, almost no metal |
| Painted metal | Cover, doors, cabinets | Mid roughness, some metal |
| Bare metal | Pipes, rails, frames | Lower roughness, clearly metal |
| Industrial floor | Seams and markings | Dark, matte |
| Energy | Core, transfer hardware | Unlit, restrained cyan or amber |
| Emissive equipment | Signs, fixture bars, status lights | Unlit, small area |

Avoid mirror-like surfaces. A little world-space color variation is enough until real tiling textures exist. A high-contrast noise texture reads as a checker and is not acceptable.

Current assets, all generated by the `VanthexBuildArt` commandlet:

- `M_VanthexSurface` — one lit shader for concrete, painted steel, bare steel, dark metal, floor, and rubber. Instances set base color, roughness, metallic, panel size, relief, wear, and a small emissive. The shader builds panel seams in world space. It is marked for Nanite and instanced meshes. If that shader fails to compile, Unreal substitutes the default material and the hall immediately looks unfinished.
- `M_VanthexEnergy` — unlit cyan or amber, with a small pulse. Used on the core chamber, pylon caps, and fixture diffusers.
- `M_VanthexGlass` — thin two-sided glass for door windows.
- `M_VanthexDecal` — deferred decal for floor lines, the core pad, and hazard stripes.
- `M_VanthexSign` — unlit bitmap lettering on a dark plate. Original words only.

There is no scanned texture library in this project. Macro variation comes from the surface shader, not from Megascans.

## Lighting

Readability beats cinematic darkness. Enemies and the core must stay visible.

- Cool industrial key from a directional light and a skylight. The lower hemisphere stays dim, not black.
- Practical sources mark the places that matter: core, both spawns, both extractions, the catwalk.
- The core area is the brightest pool. Extraction A is cyan. Extraction B is amber. The rest of the hall stays neutral.
- The ceiling is lifted with a low emissive so the room does not crush to black. Fixture bars are the visible lamps.
- Fog is light and starts at a distance. Volumetric fog is off.
- Post process stays sharp: modest bloom, no motion blur, no chromatic aberration, no crushed blacks, no heavy grain.

Keep dynamic lights few. A multiplayer match cannot afford a light on every prop.

## Ballistic rifle

The rifle is hitscan and must look ballistic. No green debug line, no laser, no cyan on the bullet. Feedback is a short warm muzzle flash, a small gray puff, an ejected shell, warm sparks on metal, dull dust on concrete, and a dark impact decal. Cyan stays on the core and on facility energy hardware. The optic lamp is a small warm dot.

There is no first-person arm mesh in the engine install. The viewmodel is the generated `SM_Rifle`. Do not invent hands.

## Vanthex Energy

Energy is cyan / blue-white, used sparingly.

Use it on the core chamber, the floor ring under the core, transfer rings, pylon caps, and a few status lights. Do not wash the concrete in blue. Extraction B uses amber so the two stations are distinguishable at a glance.

## Core

The core is a compact containment device: `SM_CoreDevice`, a dark metal cage around a bright inner chamber, with small status lights and a thin spinning ring. It has to read at rest, in a player's hands, in the air, and at distance. The cage silhouette stays simple on purpose.

A thrown core leaves a short trail of small glowing segments. The trail is there so the flight path is readable. It must not fill the screen or hide a player. Throw speed, catch radius, and the rest of the core rules are gameplay and are not changed for the look.

## Extraction

Each station is a transfer machine: `SM_TransferBase`, four `SM_Pylon` posts, a cable between the posts, a spinning charge ring, and a TRANSFER sign beside the plate. During the existing five-second hold the energy strength rises. Timing and the zone volume do not change.

- A is cyan.
- B is amber.

The plates stay on the existing zone positions. The pylons and signs do not carry collision, so they do not change who is inside the zone.

## Competitive readability

- Do not hide players behind visual clutter.
- Do not put detail on the chest-height center shot lane.
- New meshes do not collide and do not replace the gameplay blocks.
- Hazard stripes sit on the face of cover, not in the bullet path.
- Signs are large enough to read and sit off the lanes.
- Energy glow marks an objective. It does not bloom across the room.
- If a shot looks good but an enemy silhouette disappears, the shot is wrong.

## Performance

This is a multiplayer FPS prototype.

- One surface material, instanced across the kit. Nanite is on for the generated meshes. Gameplay blocks stay the engine cube.
- New dress does not collide. The ceiling slab does not cast shadows.
- Practical spots stay on the objectives, the spawns, and the catwalk. Do not add a light per prop.
- No volumetric fog, no dense combat particles, no screenshot-only light counts.
- The thrown-core trail stays a handful of small meshes.
- Auto exposure is clamped so the floor is not lifted to white against the dark ceiling.

## What this pass still is

A modular kit of constructed boxes: wall panels, columns, beams, rails, racks, cabinets, vents, doors, crates, trays, pylons, a core cage, and a transfer base. The surfaces have seams and roughness, not scanned detail. Signs are still a bitmap font. Players are block bodies. The rifle is a first-person assembly of cubes and cylinders, because no arm or weapon mesh exists in the engine install.

The next jump needs authored meshes and tiling textures: a sculpted hero core, machined transfer stations, real concrete and metal maps, a decal atlas, a proper signage font, and first-person arms. Gameplay collision stays the skeleton those meshes are built around. How the kit is built and what is missing is in `Docs/ENVIRONMENT_PIPELINE.md`.
